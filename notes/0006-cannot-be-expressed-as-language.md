There is knowledge that cannot be expressed as language. This page is a text attempt at that corpus, which is impossible: text is the one modality the corpus is about what escapes. The only move is to use language to point at where language gives out, and to be honest that the pointing is not the thing.

1. The body's skill. You can ride a bicycle, but you can't tell someone how in words; they have to fall, and learn. The knowledge is in the balance, the hands, the reflexes, none of which are sentences.
2. The senses' taste. The taste of salt is not the word "salty." A recipe is a text bridge to the taste, and the bridge is not the thing. The taste stays on the tongue.
3. The pattern eye. A chess master sees the winning line in a position; a doctor "just knows" the diagnosis. Ask them to explain and they reach for cases, not rules. The knowing is in the seeing, not the saying.
4. The trained weights. A neural network knows its task, but its knowledge is in numbers, not words. Reading the weights is not the function; the function is what the weights do, not what they say.
5. The room's mood. You walk into a room and feel the mood; you can't state it as a sentence. The feeling is in the bodies and the faces, not in a clause.
6. The unsaid. A culture runs on assumptions no one speaks: what is obvious, what is rude, what goes without saying. The knowledge is in the shared silence, and the silence is not a text.
7. The boundary. Wittgenstein ended the Tractatus with "Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent." This page is the pointing, not the silence.
